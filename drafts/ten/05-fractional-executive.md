I sell fractional engineering, so read this as somebody with an interest. I will
try to be useful anyway, mostly by being clear about when it does not work.

## The word is doing specific work

Fractional does not mean part-time, and it does not mean consultant. Both of
those describe how much time you get. Fractional describes **where the person
sits.**

A consultant looks at your business and hands you a recommendation. A contractor
takes a specification and returns an implementation. A fractional executive owns
an outcome, makes decisions, and lives with them — for a share of a week rather
than all of it.

The distinction sounds like positioning until you notice what it changes: who is
accountable when the thing does not work. A consultant's deliverable is complete
when the document is delivered. Mine is not complete until the business runs
differently.

## Why it exists now and did not before

A small business could not previously buy a fraction of a senior operator,
because a senior operator's time did not divide usefully. Too much of the week
went into context — learning the business, sitting in meetings, being available.
Below full-time, the overhead ate the contribution.

What changed is not that people got cheaper. It is that the work between the
decisions collapsed. The scheduling system, the billing integration, the audit
tooling — the parts that used to require a team to execute a senior person's
decisions can now be executed by that same person directly. The ratio of
judgement to typing shifted hard toward judgement, and judgement is the part that
was always divisible.

That is why this shows up on a careers list in 2026 and not in 2016.

## Where it fails

I will be specific, because vendors are rarely specific about this.

**It fails when the problem is headcount, not judgement.** If you know exactly
what needs building and just need more hands, you want contractors. Paying for
seniority to do work that does not require decisions is a bad trade for you.

**It fails when nobody internally owns anything.** Fractional works alongside a
business that has its own centre of gravity. If the expectation is that someone
part-time becomes the only person who knows how anything works, you have bought a
single point of failure and put it on a retainer.

**It fails when the engagement has no end state.** The honest version has a
finish: the system runs, somebody there can operate it, the retainer drops or
stops. An arrangement designed to continue indefinitely is a salary with worse
terms for both sides.

## What to ask before hiring one

Ask what happens when they leave. If the answer does not include somebody on your
team operating the thing, you are not buying a fraction of an executive. You are
renting a dependency.
