The tenth entry on that careers list was **Trades & Service Business Owner**,
sitting underneath AI product managers and climate tech founders.

It reads like filler. A list about the next decade, and the last slot goes to
plumbing.

I own one of these businesses. I also built the software it runs on, and of the
ten entries this is the one I would defend hardest.

## The receipts first

Haslett Handyman is a home-services company in Michigan. Through software I wrote
and still operate: **$216K invoiced**, **$192K collected**, **253 jobs** carried
end to end, **51 scheduled automations** running the parts nobody should do by
hand.

There are crews, because someone has to be on the roof. Everything above the work
— scheduling, quoting, invoicing, payouts, collections, the software itself — is
one person.

## Why it belongs on the list

**The work cannot be generated.** Every other entry on that list describes work
that happens on a screen, and everything that happens on a screen is now
competing with a machine that does it instantly. A blocked drain is not a
document. Nobody is prompting their way to a repaired soffit.

**The demand is local and inelastic.** It does not move offshore because the
labour got cheaper somewhere else, and it does not disappear in a downturn — it
changes shape. Houses keep failing.

**The competition is not getting better.** This is the part people miss. I have
audited **456 wellness and service websites** in Michigan. A third of the clinic
sites could not be tapped to call from a phone. The bar is not high, and it is
not rising, because the operators are busy doing the actual work.

## What changed, and it is not what you think

The reason this is a better business in 2026 than 2016 is not that AI helps you
do the work. It does not, at all.

It is that the administrative layer stopped requiring people.

The thing that kills small service businesses is almost never the work. It is
scheduling, quoting, chasing invoices, splitting crew pay by rate, and the fact
that all of it lives in one person's head and a group chat. That layer used to
demand either an office manager you could not afford or evenings you could not
spare.

It does not any more. One person can carry it now — which is the whole reason my
business works with crews in the field and no office staff.

That is the actual claim buried in entry number ten. Not that trades are hot.
That the ceiling on a one-person-owned service business just moved a long way up,
because the administrative cost of being larger collapsed while the work itself
stayed exactly as valuable and exactly as impossible to automate.

## The combination nobody on the list mentions

The ten entries are written as alternatives. Pick one.

The interesting thing is what happens when you hold two. **Trades business owner**
plus **AI automation specialist** is not two careers — it is one, and it is worth
considerably more than either alone, because the person who owns the problem is
also the person who can build the fix, with no translation layer and no budget
approval between the two.

That is not a clever positioning trick. It is just what happened to me by
accident, and it is the only reason any of the numbers at the top of this post
exist.

If you are already in the trades, the opportunity is not to leave for one of the
other nine. It is to become dangerous at the thing that eats your evenings.
