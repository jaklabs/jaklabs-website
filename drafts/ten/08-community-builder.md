I am building a developer community — a bootcamp-plus-community for people
learning the trade properly. It is real and unfinished, so this post is a
progress report rather than a lesson. I have no outcome to report yet.

What I can report is that the part I assumed would be hard is not the hard part.

## What I expected to be hard

The platform. Content, structure, assessment, the engine that routes somebody
through a track suited to their weaknesses. That is engineering, it is the thing
I am good at, and I was quietly looking forward to it.

It is a solved problem. Off-the-shelf tools do most of it, and what they do not
do I can build. Two weeks of work, generously.

## What is actually hard

Being the reason somebody comes back on a Tuesday when they are tired.

A community is not an artefact. It is a repeated behaviour by people who owe you
nothing, and it decays by default. Every week it needs a reason to exist that is
larger than the friction of showing up, and nothing about being able to build
software helps with that.

The uncomfortable version: the skills are close to opposite. Building rewards
going quiet for a long stretch and emerging with something finished. A community
punishes exactly that. Disappear for three weeks and you do not come back to a
slightly stale project; you come back to a room that has learned it can do
without you.

## The one thing I am reasonably confident about

Communities that work seem to be organised around **a thing being done**, not
around a topic.

A community about a subject is a forum, and forums die quietly. A community
around a shared activity with visible progress — people shipping, being reviewed,
being stuck in public — seems to hold, because there is a reason to return that
is not social obligation.

That is why mine is built around assessment and a route through it rather than
around lessons. Not because I have proven it works. Because when I look at the
ones that lasted, that is the property they share, and it is the only design
decision I would defend right now.

## What I would want to know before calling this a career

Whether it compounds or resets. A software system I built five years ago still
runs. Does a community I built five years ago still exist, or is it a treadmill
where stopping means it is gone in a quarter?

I genuinely do not know. Ask me in two years and I will have a real answer
instead of a hypothesis, and if the answer is that I could not sustain it, I will
write that post too.

## Where I come into this

No advice to sell here yet — I am mid-build. If you have run a community that lasted more than a couple of years, [I would like to ask you what held it together](/contact). That is a receipt I do not have and would rather borrow than guess at.
